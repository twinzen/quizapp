[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Not only it brings
CORRECT: Not only does it bring
DESCRIPTION: When you begin a clause with the restrictive phrase "Not only", you must invert the word order by adding an auxiliary verb like "does".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: it also have
CORRECT: it also has
DESCRIPTION: The singular pronoun "it" requires the singular verb form "has" rather than "have".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: sparkling fishes
CORRECT: sparkling fish
DESCRIPTION: The standard plural form for a group of fish is "fish", not "fishes".
[/MISTAKE]

CORRECT: butterflies play
DESCRIPTION: "Butterflies" is a plural noun, so it takes the plural verb "play" rather than "plays".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: busy bees collected
CORRECT: busy bees collect
DESCRIPTION: Keep your tenses consistent within the sentence; since you started in the present tense ("butterflies play"), use the present tense "collect".

DESCRIPTION: When using 'not only' at the start of a sentence, invert the subject and verb (e.g. 'Not only does it...') and ensure your verbs agree with singular nouns. This advanced structure creates an engaging, fluent rhythm for your reader.
ORIGINAL: Not only it brings me down memory lane, it also have sea turtles and sparkling fishes swimming in a warm pond.
BETTER_VERSION: Not only does it send me strolling down memory lane, but it also features a warm pond where sea turtles glide alongside sparkling fish.
[/LEARNING_POINT]
