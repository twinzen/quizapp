TITLE: Forgettable or Unforgettable Birthday
OVERALL_COMMENT: This piece shows a decent narrative structure, Keroro, but it is dragged down by atrocious spelling, careless grammar, and basic structural laziness. A Year 5 student should know how to double consonants, avoid sentence fragments, and refrain from typing in all-caps as if writing a text message. Pull your socks up and redraft this immediately.

Forgetable or Unforgetable Birthday

Keroro will never forget his nineth birthday. He desperately wanted his birthday to be perfect. Weeks before the party, he asked his friend, Dororo, to use his gargantuan mansion because it was sparkly and grand.

On the day, Dororo decorated the ballroom with green balloons. Keroro joyously skipped to Dororo’s enormous house. The smell of fruit cake danced to the hallway. The music played lively. The cute small tables piled with cupcakes and macaroon and slushie. Keroro couldn’t stop smiling.

“What?” Keroro cried. “It is two o’clock already.” He rushed to the windows to find no one waiting outside. Suddenly, Dororo brought some invatations, to find it was Keroro’s!

“Keroro! Did you forget to send the cards?” said Dororo worriedly. The party was ruined, as no one was coming to the party. Keroro’s hands were shaking and his smile disappeared.

Luckily, the ninja, Dororo, could run and jump quickly to the rescue. Together, they gave the invatations to Sumomo, Tamama, Giroro and Kululu. All the friends dashed to Dororo’s mansion to join the party.

Finally, the party started and everyone cheered loudly. They played games and danced until they got tired. It was the BEST day EVER! Looking back, Keroro realised he had to be less forgetful and realised what friends are for.
[/ORIGINAL_WRITING]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Forgetable or Unforgetable Birthday
CORRECT: Forgettable or Unforgettable Birthday
DESCRIPTION: Both words in the title are misspelled. When adding a suffix that starts with a vowel to a word ending in a single consonant after a short, stressed vowel, double the final consonant.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: his nineth birthday
CORRECT: his ninth birthday
DESCRIPTION: Drop the silent "e" when turning "nine" into its ordinal form.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Dororo brought some invatations
CORRECT: Dororo brought some invitations
DESCRIPTION: Misspelled here and again later ("gave the invatations to Sumomo..."). The word comes from "invite", spelled with an i, not an a.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: The smell of fruit cake danced to the hallway
CORRECT: The smell of fruit cake danced into the hallway
DESCRIPTION: Wrong preposition — a smell travels into or through a room, not "to" it.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: The music played lively.
CORRECT: The music played in a lively manner.
DESCRIPTION: "Lively" is an adjective, not an adverb, so it cannot describe how the music "played".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: The cute small tables piled with cupcakes and macaroon and slushie.
CORRECT: The cute small tables were piled with cupcakes and macaroon and slushie.
DESCRIPTION: Missing the finite verb "were" — as written, this is a sentence fragment rather than a complete sentence.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: macaroon and slushie
CORRECT: macaroons and slushies
DESCRIPTION: These nouns should be plural to match "cupcakes".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: to find it was Keroro’s!
CORRECT: to find they were Keroro’s!
DESCRIPTION: "Invitations" is plural, so the pronoun referring back to them must also be plural.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: The party was ruined, as no one was coming to the party.
CORRECT: The party was ruined, as no one was coming to the event.
DESCRIPTION: Repeating "party" twice in the same short sentence shows a restricted vocabulary.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: It was the BEST day EVER!
CORRECT: It was the best day he could remember.
DESCRIPTION: Capitalising whole words for emphasis is text-speak, not proper narrative prose. Show enthusiasm through precise, well-chosen adjectives instead.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Keroro realised he had to be less forgetful and realised what friends are for.
CORRECT: Keroro realised he had to be less forgetful and understood what friends are for.
DESCRIPTION: Repeating "realised" in the same concluding sentence is clumsy.
[/MISTAKE]

[REFERENCE_WRITING]
The Birthday Party That Almost Didn't Happen

Keroro would never forget his ninth birthday. For weeks, he had been counting down the days, imagining the laughter, the games and, most importantly, the enormous birthday cake. He desperately wanted everything to be perfect.

A few weeks before the party, Keroro had asked his best friend, Dororo, if he could use his gargantuan mansion. It was the perfect place: a grand ballroom, glittering chandeliers and enough space for all their friends to run around.

Finally, the big day arrived.

When Keroro stepped through the enormous front doors, his eyes widened. Green balloons floated beneath the golden ceiling, while silver ribbons sparkled in the light. Small tables stood around the ballroom, piled high with cupcakes, colourful macaroons and icy slushies. From the kitchen came the delicious smell of freshly baked fruit cake, while cheerful music filled the room.

Keroro couldn't stop smiling.

“This is going to be the best birthday ever!” he exclaimed.

But two o'clock came.

Then two-fifteen.

Then half past two.

Keroro's smile slowly faded.

“Where is everyone?” he wondered.

He hurried to the window and peered outside. The driveway was completely empty. Not a single friend was waiting at the enormous gates.

A horrible thought suddenly entered his mind.

Just then, Dororo appeared, carrying a small pile of papers.

“Keroro, did you remember to send the invitations?”

Keroro froze.

His eyes widened as he stared at the invitations in Dororo's hands.

They had never been sent.

“Oh no!” he whispered.

His hands began to shake. All the excitement that had filled him that morning disappeared, replaced by a heavy feeling in his stomach. After weeks of planning, his perfect birthday was about to become a lonely afternoon.

Luckily, Dororo didn't give up.

“Don't worry!” he cried. “We can still fix this!”

The two friends raced from house to house, delivering the invitations themselves. One by one, Sumomo, Tamama, Giroro and Kululu arrived at the mansion, breathless but smiling.

Soon, the ballroom was filled with laughter.

They played games, danced to the music and ate delicious cake until they could eat no more. Keroro looked around at his friends and smiled.

Looking back, Keroro realised that the party had not been perfect after all. The invitations had been forgotten, the guests had been late and everything had nearly gone wrong.

But perhaps that was what made it unforgettable.

He had learned an important lesson that day: sometimes, the best memories were the ones you never planned.
[/REFERENCE_WRITING]
