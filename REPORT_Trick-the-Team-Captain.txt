[MISTAKE]
WRONG: It was at Wednesday morning
CORRECT: It was on Wednesday morning
DESCRIPTION: We use “on” with days of the week. “At” is not correct here.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: stern looking
CORRECT: stern-looking
DESCRIPTION: When two words work together as an adjective before a noun, a hyphen is often needed: “a stern-looking teacher.”
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Tommy Miltons Bob
CORRECT: Tommy Milton’s Bob
DESCRIPTION: A possessive name needs an apostrophe. However, this sentence is also unclear because “Bob” seems to be an extra or incorrect name.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: a rude boy and was in her team
CORRECT: a rude boy who was in her team
DESCRIPTION: “A rude boy and was” does not fit grammatically into the sentence. Use “who was” to connect the description correctly.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: who was going to try and get the place to win
CORRECT: who was going to try to win the place
DESCRIPTION: “Try to win the place” is the clearer and more natural expression. “Get the place to win” changes the meaning.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Since no one else was in
CORRECT: Since no one else was in the running
DESCRIPTION: “Was in” is unclear here. “Was in the running” clearly means that nobody else was competing for the position.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: As Summer’s special BFF, who was called Alice, helped her practice
CORRECT: As Summer’s special BFF, Alice, helped her practise
DESCRIPTION: The original sentence is awkward because “who was called Alice” is unnecessary. “Alice” can simply be placed between commas. In British English, “practise” is the verb used for practising a skill.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Alice cried “Where is Summer?”
CORRECT: Alice cried, “Where is Summer?”
DESCRIPTION: A comma should usually be placed before direct speech after a reporting verb such as “cried”.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Tommy made a sly smile
CORRECT: Tommy gave a sly smile
DESCRIPTION: We normally say someone “gave” or “smiled slyly”; we do not usually say someone “made a sly smile.”
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Alice shouted “She’s really sick.”
CORRECT: Alice shouted, “She’s really sick.”
DESCRIPTION: A comma is needed before the direct speech.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: annouced
CORRECT: announced
DESCRIPTION: “Announced” has two “n”s after the “a”. This is a spelling mistake.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: labled
CORRECT: labelled
DESCRIPTION: The correct British English spelling is “labelled”, with two “l”s and two “l”s in the middle/end pattern.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Amile
CORRECT: Amile
DESCRIPTION: This name may be intended to be “Amile”, so it is not necessarily a spelling mistake. However, make sure character names remain consistent and are clearly introduced.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: bloned
CORRECT: blonde

CORRECT: shouted, “Tommy made her sick.”

DESCRIPTION: “Summer won” is grammatically possible, but the ending is too vague. Naming what she won makes the conclusion clearer.

[REFERENCE_WRITING]
Trick the Team Captain

It was Wednesday morning when the stern-looking class teacher asked who wanted to be the team captain. Summer, a girl with bright pink hair, nervously bit her lip. She hated speaking in front of thousands of people, but she desperately wanted to stop Tommy Miltons Bob, a rude boy from her team, from getting the chance to win the place for everyone. Since nobody else volunteered, Summer knew she would have to try.

As Summer’s best friend, Alice, helped her practise, Tommy called out, “I’ll let you win.” Alice nodded, but Summer narrowed her eyes. “If this is one of his tricks, he won’t fool me,” she thought. The weekend passed in a blur, and before she knew it, the big day had arrived.

A large group of children lined up outside the hall, whispering excitedly. Strangely, the only person inside was Tommy. Alice looked around anxiously. “Where is Summer?” she cried. Tommy gave a sly smile. Suddenly, Alice realised what had happened. “Tommy, I should have known it was you!” she shouted. “She’s really sick.”

The hall door opened, and Mrs Cherry, a kind-hearted teacher, came rushing in. She announced, “Summer is sick.” Just then, a small bottle labelled “sick” slipped from Tommy’s pocket and clattered onto the floor. Amile, a girl with blonde hair, pointed at it and shouted, “Tommy made her sick!”

Whispers spread through the crowd as everyone realised what Tommy had done. A few days later, the results were announced. Summer had won! Tommy faced the consequences of his cruel trick, while Summer received more votes from the other children. From that day on, Tommy stopped being mean.
[/REFERENCE_WRITING]

DESCRIPTION: Use more precise vocabulary instead of simple words such as “big”, “nice” or “bad”. Specific word choices make your writing more vivid and interesting.

BETTER_VERSION: A large group of children lined up outside the hall, whispering excitedly.
[/LEARNING_POINT]

[LEARNING_POINT]
DESCRIPTION: Try using varied sentence openers to make your paragraphs flow more naturally. Starting sentences in different ways helps avoid a repetitive rhythm.

BETTER_VERSION: The weekend passed in a blur, and before she knew it, the big day had arrived.
[/LEARNING_POINT]

[/LEARNING_POINT]
