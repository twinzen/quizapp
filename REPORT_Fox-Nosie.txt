[MISTAKE]
WRONG: A 11 years old girl
CORRECT: An 11-year-old girl
DESCRIPTION: Use "An" before words that begin with a vowel sound, and hyphenate "11-year-old" when it is used before a noun.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: who is called Spring.
CORRECT: who was called Spring.
DESCRIPTION: Since your story is written in the past tense, maintain consistency by using "was" instead of "is".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: a 一marshmallow-pink mat.
CORRECT: a marshmallow-pink mat.
DESCRIPTION: Remove the stray character in front of "marshmallow".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: a small scatch
CORRECT: a small scratch
DESCRIPTION: The word "scratch" requires an 'r' after the letter 'c'.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: There was it again.
CORRECT: There it was again.
DESCRIPTION: In English, the pronoun "it" should come before the verb "was" in this phrase.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: layed in silent,
CORRECT: lay in silence,
DESCRIPTION: The past tense of "lie" (to recline) is "lay", and the preposition "in" must be followed by the noun "silence" rather than the adjective "silent".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: A wild crieing
CORRECT: A wild crying
DESCRIPTION: When forming the present participle of "cry", keep the 'y' and add "-ing".
[/MISTAKE]

CORRECT: echoed through the silent hall.
DESCRIPTION: The verb "echoed" needs a preposition like "through" or "in" to show how sound travels through a room.

[MISTAKE]

CORRECT: crashed through the door.
DESCRIPTION: Use the preposition "through" rather than "in" when entering forcefully across a doorway.

[/MISTAKE]

[REFERENCE_WRITING]
Summer shared a snug bedroom with her twin sister, Spring, whose clever mind was always one step ahead of her own. Silver moonlight streamed through the window, illuminating the polished wooden floorboards and resting gently on the soft, marshmallow-pink rug beneath their bunk bed.

All of a sudden, a faint scratch rattled against the windowpane, followed by a sharp squeak. Startled, Summer bolted upright. “What was that?” she whimpered, clutching her duvet.

Spring shrugged, though her eyes were wide with tension. There it was again. Both girls lay in breathless silence, wide awake and waiting.

An eerie, wild wail echoed through the quiet house. “Ahh!” shrieked Spring, jumping up. “What is that?” They both tiptoed nervously to the window, Summer’s legs trembling with every step. Outside, the pitch-black sky seemed to envelop the garden in mystery.

They decided to call their brave dad. Moments later, Dad burst through the door. “What is it?” he asked, looking around in confusion.

“We heard a cry!” said Summer, her voice wobbling. For a second, total silence returned. Then, out of nowhere, the shrill wail echoed through the frosty air once again.

“Listen,” whispered their dad softly, “I think it’s just a fox.”

“Really?” Spring asked nervously. Dad nodded with a reassuring smile.

Relieved, the girls snuggled deep into their cosy covers and squeezed their eyes shut. Though they tossed and turned for a few minutes, the steady rhythm of their breathing soon drifted them off to sleep.
[/REFERENCE_WRITING]
