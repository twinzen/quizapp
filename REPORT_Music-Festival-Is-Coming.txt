TITLE: Alice's Music Festival

OVERALL_COMMENT: You have created a clear and enjoyable story with a strong beginning, middle and ending. I especially like the way you show Alice's nervousness through details such as her stomach feeling like a knot. To raise your mark fastest, check your spelling carefully and watch for irregular past-tense verbs such as “rang” and “announced”; also try to vary your sentence openings a little more.

[ORIGINAL_WRITING]

A beautiful tune danced down the hallway. Alice Chan, a violinist with dark turquoise hair, woke up by her sister, Crystal, who was playing the flute. Why was she playing it so early? She wondered. Her brain swirled until it remembered. Of course! It was the music festival at school.

“Are you nervous?” asked Alice’s mum.

“Yes,” responed Crystal.

Alice answered, “A bit.”

As the school came in sight, they could hear the chattering of nervous children. “Brring!” The bell had rang. Alice’s stomach felt like it twisted itself into a knot. She imagined lots of teachers, students and families watching her. If she lost, she would let her families down.

She knew she had to try. The performances passed like a blur. Soon, it was her go. She stepped on the stage, closed her eyes and started playing Trivosky 3rd movement in D major on the violin. People started cheering. In a few minutes, Alice couldn’t stop smiling.

It was time to announce the winners. She bit her lip. “The winner is …… Alice Chan!” annouced the head teacher. Alice’s eyes filled with tears of joy. She had won! She knew all along that practice makes perfect.

[/ORIGINAL_WRITING]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: woke up by her sister

CORRECT: woke up to her sister

DESCRIPTION: We normally say someone “wakes up to” a sound or activity that is happening. “Woke up by” would usually mean that someone physically woke Alice.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: “Why was she playing it so early? She wondered.

CORRECT: “Why was she playing it so early?” she wondered.

DESCRIPTION: The question mark belongs inside the quotation marks because it is part of the question. “She wondered” should not begin with a capital letter because it continues the same sentence.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: “Yes,” responed Crystal.

CORRECT: “Yes,” responded Crystal.

DESCRIPTION: “Responed” is a spelling mistake. The correct past-tense spelling is “responded”.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: The bell had rang.

CORRECT: The bell had rung.

DESCRIPTION: After “had”, you need the past participle. The verb “ring” changes to “rang” in the simple past but “rung” after “had”.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: Alice’s stomach felt like it twisted itself into a knot.

CORRECT: Alice’s stomach felt as if it had twisted itself into a knot.

DESCRIPTION: After “felt like”, this sentence sounds awkward because “like” is followed by an action clause. “Felt as if it had twisted itself into a knot” makes the comparison clearer and more natural.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: she would let her families down.

CORRECT: she would let her family down.

DESCRIPTION: “Family” is normally used as a collective noun here. You are talking about Alice’s family as one group, so “family” is correct.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: Soon, it was her go.

CORRECT: Soon, it was her turn.

DESCRIPTION: The usual English expression is “it was her turn”. “It was her go” is understandable in some varieties of English, but “her turn” is the more natural choice for formal school writing.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: started playing Trivosky 3rd movement

CORRECT: started playing Tchaikovsky's 3rd movement

DESCRIPTION: “Trivosky” appears to be a spelling mistake for the composer's name “Tchaikovsky”. When naming a particular composer's movement, an apostrophe can also make the meaning clearer.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: “The winner is …… Alice Chan!” annouced

CORRECT: “The winner is … Alice Chan!” announced

DESCRIPTION: “Annouced” is misspelled; the correct spelling is “announced”. You also only need one ellipsis (three dots) rather than six dots.

[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]

WRONG: practice makes perfect.

CORRECT: practice makes perfect.

DESCRIPTION: This sentence is grammatically correct. However, because the story is mainly narrated in the past tense, “She knew all along that practice makes perfect” works because this is a general saying that remains true. No correction is needed here.

[/MISTAKE]


[REFERENCE_WRITING]
Music Festival is Coming

A beautiful tune danced down the hallway. Alice Chan, a violinist with dark turquoise hair, woke to the gentle sound of her sister, Crystal, playing the flute beside her. Why was she playing so early? Alice wondered sleepily. Her thoughts swirled around her foggy mind until, suddenly, she remembered. Of course! It was the school music festival.

“Are you nervous?” asked Alice’s mum.

“Yes,” responded Crystal nervously.

Alice smiled. “A bit.”

As the school came into sight, Alice could hear the chattering of nervous children all around her. “Brrring!” The bell rang loudly, making her jump. Suddenly, her stomach twisted itself into a tight knot. She imagined rows of teachers, students and families watching her from the audience. What if she lost? She would let her family down.

Taking a deep breath, Alice reminded herself that she had practised for this moment. She knew she had to try her best.

The performances passed in a blur of music and applause. Soon, it was her turn. With trembling hands, she stepped onto the stage. She closed her eyes for a moment, took one final breath and began playing Tchaikovsky's Third Movement in D major on her violin. The beautiful melody floated through the hall. As she reached the final note, the audience burst into loud applause. Alice opened her eyes and could not stop smiling.

Finally, it was time to announce the winners. Alice nervously bit her lip as the head teacher stepped forward.

“The winner is... Alice Chan!” announced the head teacher.

For a moment, Alice stood completely still. Then, her eyes filled with tears of joy. She had won! All those hours of practice had been worth it. At last, she understood that hard work and determination really could make a difference.
[/REFERENCE_WRITING]
