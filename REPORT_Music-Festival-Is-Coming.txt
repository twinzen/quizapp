Music Festival is Coming

A beautiful tune danced down the
hallway. Alice Chan, a violinist with dark turquoise hair, woke up by her sister,
Crystal, who was playing the flute. Why was she playing it so early? She
wondered. Her brain swirled until it remembered. Of course! It was the music
festival at school.

“Are you nervous? asked Alice’s mum.

“Yes,” responed Crystal.

Alice answered, “A bit.”

As the school came in sight, they could
hear the chattering of nervous children. “Brring!” The bell had rang. Alice’s
stomach felt like it twisted itself into a knot. She imagined lots of teachers,
students and families watching her. If her lost, she would let her families
down.

She knew she had to try. The performances
passed like a blur. Soon, it was her go. She stepped on the stage, closed her
eyes and started playing Trivosky 3rd movement in D major on the violin.
People started cheering. In a few minutes, Alice couldn’t stop smiling.

It was time to announce the winners. She
bit her lip. “The winner is …… Alice Chan!” annouced the head teacher. Alice’s
eyes filled with tears of joy. She had won! She knew all along that practice
makes perfect.
[/ORIGINAL_WRITING]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: woke up by her sister
CORRECT: woke up to the sound of her sister
DESCRIPTION: “Woke up by” is not the natural expression here. Use “woke up to the sound of” to show that the flute playing woke Alice.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: “Are you nervous? asked Alice’s mum.
CORRECT: “Are you nervous?” asked Alice’s mum.
DESCRIPTION: The question mark must come before the closing quotation mark because the question is part of the spoken words.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: responed
CORRECT: responded
DESCRIPTION: “Responded” is the correct spelling. Remember that the word contains “nd” before the ending.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: The bell had rang.
CORRECT: The bell had rung.
DESCRIPTION: After “had”, you need the past participle. The past participle of “ring” is “rung”, not “rang”.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: If her lost
CORRECT: If she lost
DESCRIPTION: “She” is the subject pronoun needed here. “Her” is used as an object or possessive word.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: let her families down
CORRECT: let her family down
DESCRIPTION: “Family” is being used as a group here, so it should be singular. The usual expression is “let her family down”.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Soon, it was her go.
CORRECT: Soon, it was her turn.
DESCRIPTION: “It was her turn” is the natural English expression when someone is about to perform.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: Trivosky
CORRECT: Tchaikovsky
DESCRIPTION: If you mean the famous Russian composer Tchaikovsky, his name is spelled “Tchaikovsky”. Be especially careful with the spelling of names and titles you have learned.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: annouced
CORRECT: announced
DESCRIPTION: “Announced” is the correct spelling. The word has “ann” in the middle.
[/MISTAKE]

[REFERENCE_WRITING]
Music Festival is Coming

A beautiful tune danced down the hallway. Alice Chan, a violinist with dark turquoise hair, woke to the gentle sound of her sister, Crystal, playing the flute beside her. Why was she playing so early? Alice wondered sleepily. Her thoughts swirled around her foggy mind until, suddenly, she remembered. Of course! It was the school music festival.

“Are you nervous?” asked Alice’s mum.

“Yes,” responded Crystal nervously.

Alice smiled. “A bit.”

As the school came into sight, Alice could hear the chattering of nervous children all around her. “Brrring!” The bell rang loudly, making her jump. Suddenly, her stomach twisted itself into a tight knot. She imagined rows of teachers, students and families watching her from the audience. What if she lost? She would let her family down.

Taking a deep breath, Alice reminded herself that she had practised for this moment. She knew she had to try her best.

The performances passed in a blur of music and applause. Soon, it was her turn. With trembling hands, she stepped onto the stage. She closed her eyes for a moment, took one final breath and began playing Tchaikovsky's Third Movement in D major on her violin. The beautiful melody floated through the hall. As she reached the final note, the audience burst into loud applause. Alice opened her eyes and could not stop smiling.

Finally, it was time to announce the winners. Alice nervously bit her lip as the head teacher stepped forward.

“The winner is... Alice Chan!” announced the head teacher.

For a moment, Alice stood completely still. Then, her eyes filled with tears of joy. She had won! All those hours of practice had been worth it. At last, she understood that hard work and determination really could make a difference.
[/REFERENCE_WRITING]
