[MISTAKE]
WRONG: rich getalmans and women
CORRECT: rich gentlemen and women
DESCRIPTION: "Getalmans" is a misspelling of "gentlemen". The plural of "man" is "men", so "gentlemen" ends with "-men".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: extremly difficult
CORRECT: extremely difficult
DESCRIPTION: Remember to keep the 'e' when adding '-ly' to "extreme" to spell "extremely".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: a handful of violinist
CORRECT: a handful of violinists
DESCRIPTION: After the phrase "a handful of", you need the plural noun "violinists" instead of singular "violinist".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: play it.
CORRECT: play them.
DESCRIPTION: You are referring back to the plural noun "pieces", so use the plural pronoun "them" rather than "it".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: I want to meet him is because
CORRECT: The reason I want to meet him is that
DESCRIPTION: Writing "I want to meet him is because" creates an awkward sentence structure. Use either "I want to meet him because..." or "The reason I want to meet him is that...".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: I learn the violin
CORRECT: I am learning the violin
DESCRIPTION: To express an action that is currently ongoing in your life, use the present continuous form "I am learning" rather than "I learn".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: I alway want
CORRECT: I have always wanted
DESCRIPTION: "Alway" is missing the final 's' for "always". Using "have always wanted" also creates a much smoother present perfect sentence.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: some impossible skill
CORRECT: some impossible skills
DESCRIPTION: The determiner "some" here refers to multiple techniques, so "skill" should be pluralized to "skills".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: if I see him play from one metre will be even to make me extremely exited
CORRECT: seeing him play from one metre away would be enough to make me extremely excited
DESCRIPTION: This sentence needs a noun phrase like "seeing him play" as its subject, and "enough" instead of "even". Also, "excited" is spelled with a 'c'.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: “exited emotion”
CORRECT: "excited emotion"
DESCRIPTION: "Exited" means left a place, while "excited" (spelled with a 'c') describes a feeling of enthusiasm.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: rumors about he can do
CORRECT: rumours that he could do
DESCRIPTION: Follow "rumours" with "that..." when introducing a full clause. Note also that in British English, "rumours" is spelled with a 'u'.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: controlled by devil
CORRECT: controlled by the devil
DESCRIPTION: Singular nouns like "devil" require an article like "the" before them.
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: when he play!
CORRECT: when he plays!
DESCRIPTION: Present-tense verbs with third-person singular subjects ("he") need an 's' on the end, so it should be "he plays".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: died hundreds of years before.
CORRECT: died hundreds of years ago.
DESCRIPTION: Use "ago" when counting back time from the present moment rather than "before".
[/MISTAKE]

[MISTAKE]
WRONG: which only a few who can play it.
CORRECT: which only a few can play.
DESCRIPTION: Including "who" makes this clause incomplete. Removing "who" forms a proper relative clause linked to "pieces".
[/MISTAKE]

WRONG: play it too!
CORRECT: play them too!
DESCRIPTION: Use the plural pronoun "them" to match the plural noun "pieces" you mentioned earlier.

Paganini

Paganini was a virtuoso violinist who captivated wealthy aristocracy in the nineteenth century with his extraordinary musical talent. Throughout his legendary career, he composed groundbreaking pieces that pushed the instrument to its absolute limits—so much so that only a handful of master musicians today can perform them effortlessly. Although many of his compositions remain notoriously difficult, a few accessible works still allow young musicians to experience his genius.

As an aspiring violinist who is constantly striving to improve, I would give anything to meet this musical genius in person. I imagine him demonstrating one of his seemingly impossible techniques, unlocking secrets that no modern tutor could ever teach me. Even just standing a metre away and watching his fingers dance across the fingerboard would fill me with electrifying thrill—that exact, restless joy you feel on the eve of your birthday! Beyond his incredible skill, I am fascinated by the wild legends surrounding him: people whispered that he could stretch three octaves at once and even claimed he was possessed by the devil when he played.

Sadly, Paganini passed away centuries ago, making a face-to-face meeting impossible. Nevertheless, his breathtaking music endures, serving as an ultimate test for the world's finest violinists. One day, with enough dedication and practice, I hope I will be able to play his masterworks too.
[/REFERENCE_WRITING]
